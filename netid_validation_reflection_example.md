
# Validation Reflection - BJY819 #

Most of our disagreements came from looking at different time ranges. I used the full monthly history, 
while my teammate sometimes looked at just the recent GitHub activity. A project could look steady 
recently but declining over its whole history.

Comparing our reasoning helped us settle some of these differences. 
For IAMR, we settled on irregular because the full history had a large peak followed by a drop. 
Some disagreements still need discussion, especially what counts as recovery and how to label commit themes.

The biggest thing I learned is that a gap does not always mean a project is dead. 
Work can pick back up with fixes or new features, but a couple of commits do not 
necessarily mean the project is fully active again. For example, kymatio_wph had 
only two commits after a 30-month gap. Commit messages helped show what changed, 
but often did not explain why people stopped or returned.
